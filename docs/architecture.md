# Streaming architecture

Ingestion accumulates at most 512 samples plus an unmatched byte. Internal processing blocks are independent of read boundaries. A bounded reader queue provides backpressure. State remains live while delivery pauses; timers for signal loss advance from PCM silence, not wall time.

The default demodulator is an independently implemented linear-phase complex FIR bandpass centered at 1900 Hz. A narrower parallel path feeds protocol detection. At acquisition, reference-tone errors select the narrower pixel path if it substantially improves reception; otherwise the broad path preserves detail. Images acquired on the broad path continue comparing inner-sync tone errors at row boundaries. A three-reference median selects narrow filtering when its error is substantially lower; eight consecutive clean broad-path references restore the wider path. Acquisition-selected narrow filtering and explicit narrow mode remain fixed for the image. Both continuously running histories have identical compensated sample timelines, so switching preserves the line clock and adds no audio delay. A selectable alternative uses a real FIR band limiter followed by a Hilbert FIR. All paths preserve convolution history, discriminate analytic phase sample by sample, and subtract exact FIR group delay from the absolute frequency sample coordinates. No resampling is required.

Protocol tone bins use robust short temporal aggregation separate from pixel apertures. Acquisition validates both leaders, break, start, seven LSB-first bits, even parity, and stop. Header-free acquisition needs three sync observations matching cadence and offset plus porch and channel evidence. Unrelated candidate pulses can be skipped rather than derailing the cadence chain. Search tolerates offset; once acquired, expected tone and timing windows constrain updates.

Robot36 headerless acquisition can validate a rejected porch directly from raw PCM: the central two milliseconds must strongly fit 1500 Hz, explain at least 60% of centered energy, dominate competing 1200/1900 Hz fits by at least twofold, and exceed the minimum amplitude. All cadence, duration, offset-agreement, and alternating-separator gates still apply. This handles short filtered-FM transients without globally loosening tone tolerances. Headerless pixel-band selection compares median reference errors across the three acquisition pulses rather than trusting one noisy pulse.

Frequency reference observations and line timing estimates remain separate. Leader/sync measurements update an offset only under confidence and bounded-change checks. A weighted robust line-index/sample-position regression rejects outliers; smoothed fitted period and phase position subsequent scans fractionally. Timing confidence includes weighted residual jitter, not just the number of accepted pulses. Once a consistent fit is established, marginal observations are rejected and inconsistent fits retain the learned clock. Ordinary phase updates are limited to ten microseconds per observation; sustained low-jitter evidence can reacquire a genuine clock change. After acquisition, raw-audio matched-tone correlation searches a bounded local window for sync when instantaneous phase is unreliable. Short missed-sync intervals use prediction and explicit recovered flags. Longer loss closes a recoverable partial segment. The model does not independently reposition every row to its last noisy edge.

Frequency correction prefers qualified raw constant-tone fits, including DC, over filtered phase medians. Each fit searches a bounded neighborhood and must explain at least 90% of centered energy; boundary minima and weak tones are rejected. At least two qualified acquisition references establish a refined headerless offset. Subsequent updates use a bounded sixteen-reference median and smooth the result, with confidence aggregated across the qualified observations. The prior phase estimator remains a fallback when no qualified raw references are available. Raw-reference fit quality is not a calibrated frequency-error bound.

Pixel extraction integrates a short aperture within each pixel and interpolates fractional positions. Phase observations are combined as circular adjacent-sample correlations weighted by their analytic amplitude products. Fractional reads crossing a phase wrap or amplitude null interpolate complex products before taking their angle, avoiding false tones manufactured by linear interpolation of wrapped Hz values. Ordinary smooth segments retain the existing estimator. Samples near amplitude nulls therefore have little leverage; their unstable phase no longer receives full weight. Adaptive sine fitting evaluates the raw samples only within individual pixel windows; over 90% of a channel must meet a stringent residual threshold before the clean-signal path is used, and individual failed fits retain phase estimates. Its fit confidence describes residual, not frequency certainty.

Automatic noise reduction measures unexplained raw PCM energy in a five-millisecond constant-tone reference. A ratio above 0.02 enables a five-pixel local Wiener estimate for non-clean-fit channels: local variance estimates noise power, and higher-contrast edges retain more of their original estimate. Constant-tone references establish noisy reception independently of picture texture; received sync observations update the reference estimate. Clean fitted channels bypass smoothing. Filtering adds no future-row dependency or audio latency. This is a conservative initial algorithm, not a claim of optimal denoising or a calibrated RF SNR.

The same reference-noise gate enables a separate half-strength chroma refinement, leaving Y unchanged. It uses five native chroma positions with weights constrained by both Cr/Cb differences and every intervening luminance edge. Robot retains its 160-sample chroma until refinement; both horizontal luminance parities and both received rows guide a complete pair before chroma is expanded. PD uses its full-width shared Cr/Cb once for both rows. Its first row still emits after Cb using the first Y guide; the second Y can refine the shared chroma and revise the first row. No luminance from one row replaces another row's samples, and damage to PD's first Y does not lower the second row's quality.

Optional impulse rejection uses robust frequency-aperture sampling. A manual noise-reduction override replaces automatic treatment with a three-sample median blend, using the selected strength for chroma and 35% of that strength for luminance. Explicit zero disables both automatic and manual treatment. Display brightness/contrast/gamma/saturation are separate from protocol conversion.

Robot 36 uses the documented 9+3+88+4.5+1.5+44 ms scan structure. 320 luminance pixels remain sharp; 160 chroma samples are reconstructed horizontally. Raw separator-tone projections determine Cr/Cb phase, with phase-frequency measurements as a fallback. If the VIS establishes the beginning, missing phase can be inferred from protocol parity and is flagged. Headerless reception does not assume original row parity. Adjacent even-Cr/odd-Cb scans share chroma; the provisional even row is replaced once Cb arrives. First/last unpaired rows use explicit provisional chroma.

PD120 uses 20 ms sync, 2.080 ms porch, and four 121.600 ms full-width channels: first Y, Cr, Cb, second Y. Shared chroma produces two output rows per physical pair. The first row emits immediately after Cb; the second emits after its Y scan. No Robot chroma-parity assumptions enter PD extraction.

BT.601 limited-range YCrCb conversion follows Appendix B of the [Dayton specification](https://www.classicsstv.com/downloads/daytonpaper.pdf). This primary document gives explicit Robot boundaries, which differ from aggregated timings in some handbook tables. No existing decoder implementation was read or copied.

Ring buffers retain at most 2.5 seconds each of picture frequencies/envelopes, protocol frequencies/envelopes, and raw PCM for local fitting. The protocol buffer shares the picture buffer when the explicitly selected demodulator is already narrow. Filter state, protocol bins/candidates, at most one PD chroma pair, and fixed-size authoritative image rows are retained. Robot component state is capped by 240 rows; PD image state by 496 rows. Full raw recordings are only retained by the development corpus/tools, never indefinitely by the streaming process. Processing is proportional to new audio, with optional per-channel fits; no repeated whole-recording decode or end-of-image refinement is required.
