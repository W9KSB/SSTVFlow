# Streaming architecture

Ingestion accumulates at most 512 samples plus an unmatched byte. Internal processing blocks are independent of read boundaries. A bounded reader queue provides backpressure. State remains live while delivery pauses; timers for signal loss advance from PCM silence, not wall time.

The default demodulator is an independently implemented linear-phase complex FIR bandpass centered at 1900 Hz. A narrower parallel path feeds protocol detection. At acquisition, reference-tone errors select the narrower pixel path if it substantially improves reception; otherwise the broad path preserves detail. Images acquired on the broad path continue comparing inner-sync tone errors at row boundaries. A three-reference median selects narrow filtering when its error is substantially lower; eight consecutive clean broad-path references restore the wider path. Acquisition-selected narrow filtering and explicit narrow mode remain fixed for the image. Explicit sharp mode uses a 750 Hz low-pass prototype centered at 1900 Hz (nominal 1150-2650 Hz), compared with 950 Hz for narrow and 1450 Hz for broad. It fixes the picture path while the normal narrow path continues protocol detection. All three use the same four-millisecond FIR length and delay compensation; sharp adds no additional retained history. Sharp filtering can suppress noise at the cost of fine detail and is never selected automatically. Both continuously running histories have identical compensated sample timelines, so switching preserves the line clock and adds no audio delay. A selectable alternative uses a real FIR band limiter followed by a Hilbert FIR. All paths preserve convolution history, discriminate analytic phase sample by sample, and subtract exact FIR group delay from the absolute frequency sample coordinates. No resampling is required.

Protocol tone bins use robust short temporal aggregation separate from pixel apertures. Acquisition validates both leaders, break, start, seven LSB-first bits, even parity, and stop. Reference evidence is bounded even during sustained tones: second leaders expire after 365 milliseconds, and sync evidence retains at most 32 one-millisecond bins while preserving the original tone start so a long tone cannot become invented short syncs. Header-free acquisition normally needs three sync observations matching cadence and offset plus porch and channel evidence. PD180 can qualify two syncs when the intervening complete pair has valid video-band evidence and both raw sync tones pass a stronger fit check; its slower cadence can otherwise require more than a two-second excerpt. Unrelated candidate pulses can be skipped rather than derailing the cadence chain. Search tolerates offset; once acquired, expected tone and timing windows constrain updates.

Robot36 headerless acquisition can validate a rejected porch directly from raw PCM: the central two milliseconds must strongly fit 1500 Hz, explain at least 60% of centered energy, dominate competing 1200/1900 Hz fits by at least twofold, and exceed the minimum amplitude. The normal acquisition path retains its cadence, duration, offset-agreement, and alternating-separator gates. Headerless pixel-band selection compares median reference errors across acquisition pulses rather than trusting one noisy pulse.

When filtered sync pulses cannot establish Robot36 or Robot72, a bounded raw-PCM fallback checks coherent tone energy across four physical lines. Their cadence, frequency offset, video-band occupancy, and chroma markers must agree under a common timing adjustment. Robot36 checks alternating 1500/2300 Hz separators; Robot72 checks both markers on every line and rejects extra sync pulses within its 300 ms line, which can indicate a faster unsupported mode. This path tolerates fragmented noisy syncs and extended captured sync tails without accepting malformed VIS. Images acquired through it use local raw leading-edge evidence for subsequent timing updates, and remain partial because their original beginning is unknown.

Frequency reference observations and line timing estimates remain separate. Leader/sync measurements update an offset only under confidence and bounded-change checks. A weighted robust line-index/sample-position regression rejects outliers; smoothed fitted period and phase position subsequent scans fractionally. Timing confidence includes weighted residual jitter, not just the number of accepted pulses. Once a consistent fit is established, marginal observations are rejected and inconsistent fits retain the learned clock. Ordinary phase updates are limited to ten microseconds per observation; sustained low-jitter evidence can reacquire a genuine clock change. After acquisition, a continuous-phase raw sync-to-porch model fits amplitude, phase and DC within a bounded local window. It locates the trailing sync boundary without depending on the preceding picture tone. At a VIS start, this boundary establishes the first row before pixels are emitted, supporting both separate first syncs and syncs merged into VIS stop. Regular long-sync tracking also compares a precise local boundary with full-pulse evidence to reject unrelated tone transitions. Weak long-sync receptions retain bounded integrated timing when the short boundary is unreliable; widened startup searches require a precise boundary. Sustained tones without a qualified boundary are rejected. Short missed-sync intervals use prediction and explicit recovered flags. Longer loss closes a recoverable partial segment. The model does not independently reposition every row to its last noisy edge.

Frequency correction prefers qualified raw constant-tone fits, including DC, over filtered phase medians. Each fit searches a bounded neighborhood and must explain at least 90% of centered energy; boundary minima and weak tones are rejected. Short Robot24 and Martin syncs use shorter centered reference windows so porch or video samples do not contaminate frequency and noise estimates. At least two qualified acquisition references establish a refined headerless offset. Subsequent updates use a bounded sixteen-reference median and smooth the result, with confidence aggregated across the qualified observations. The prior phase estimator remains a fallback when no qualified raw references are available. Raw-reference fit quality is not a calibrated frequency-error bound.

Pixel extraction integrates a short aperture within each pixel and interpolates fractional positions. Phase observations are combined as circular adjacent-sample correlations weighted by their analytic amplitude products. Fractional reads crossing a phase wrap or amplitude null interpolate complex products before taking their angle, avoiding false tones manufactured by linear interpolation of wrapped Hz values. Ordinary smooth segments retain the existing estimator. Samples near amplitude nulls therefore have little leverage; their unstable phase no longer receives full weight. Adaptive sine fitting evaluates raw samples only within individual pixel windows. Qualified leader and sync fits provide local DC estimates on the input timeline. Pixel fits must remain stable when that DC estimate is perturbed by its measured uncertainty. In flat picture regions, an independently stable phase estimate also rejects conflicting raw fits, guarding against DC changes after the reference tone. Over 90% of a channel must meet the residual and stability checks before the clean-signal path is used. Individual unstable or failed fits retain phase estimates; an unconstrained DC term is never fitted to three-sample pixel windows.

Automatic noise reduction measures unexplained raw PCM energy in a constant-tone reference. A ratio above 0.02 enables a five-pixel local Wiener estimate for non-clean-fit channels. Local correlations at several pixel spacings protect recurring detail before estimating noise from unprotected positions. Clean fitted channels and protected detail bypass smoothing. The reference residual establishes noisy reception independently of picture texture; it is not treated as a calibrated conversion to pixel-frequency variance. Filtering adds no future-row dependency or audio latency. Correlated interference can also receive conservative detail protection.

The same reference-noise gate enables a separate half-strength chroma refinement, leaving Y unchanged. It uses five native chroma positions with weights constrained by both Cr/Cb differences and every intervening luminance edge. Recurring chroma detail is protected even when it has no corresponding luminance edge. Robot retains its 160-sample chroma until refinement; both horizontal luminance parities and both received rows guide a complete pair before chroma is expanded. PD uses its full-width shared Cr/Cb once for both rows. Its first row still emits after Cb using the first Y guide; the second Y can refine the shared chroma and revise the first row. No luminance from one row replaces another row's samples, and damage to PD's first Y does not lower the second row's quality.

Optional impulse rejection uses robust frequency-aperture sampling. A manual noise-reduction override replaces automatic treatment with a three-sample median blend, using the selected strength for chroma and 35% of that strength for luminance. Explicit zero disables both automatic and manual treatment. Display brightness/contrast/gamma/saturation are separate from protocol conversion.

Robot 36 uses the documented 9+3+88+4.5+1.5+44 ms scan structure. 320 luminance pixels remain sharp; 160 chroma samples are reconstructed horizontally. Raw separator-tone projections determine Cr/Cb phase, with phase-frequency measurements as a fallback. If the VIS establishes the beginning, missing phase can be inferred from protocol parity and is flagged. Headerless reception does not assume original row parity. Adjacent even-Cr/odd-Cb scans share chroma; the provisional even row is replaced once Cb arrives. First/last unpaired rows use explicit provisional chroma.

PD120 uses 20 ms sync, 2.080 ms porch, and four 121.600 ms full-width channels: first Y, Cr, Cb, second Y. Shared chroma produces two output rows per physical pair. The first row emits immediately after Cb; the second emits after its Y scan. No Robot chroma-parity assumptions enter PD extraction.

PD180 uses the same two-row layout with 183.040 ms channel scans. Robot72 carries Y, Cr, and Cb on every physical line, with 320 luminance pixels and 160 native chroma samples; Robot24 uses 160 luminance pixels and 80 native chroma samples. These modes do not pair complementary chroma from adjacent rows as Robot36 does. MartinM1 and MartinM2 transmit green, blue, and red channels directly, with 320 pixels per channel and 256 rows. Mode profiles supply the VIS code, geometry, sync duration, cadence, and channel boundaries used by acquisition and extraction.

BT.601 limited-range YCrCb conversion follows Appendix B of the [Dayton specification](https://www.classicsstv.com/downloads/daytonpaper.pdf). This primary document gives explicit Robot36/Robot72 boundaries, which differ from aggregated timings in some handbook tables. The reception and extraction paths are independently implemented; timing references establish the transmitted layout.

Ring buffers retain at most 2.5 seconds each of picture frequencies/envelopes, protocol frequencies/envelopes, and raw PCM for local fitting. The protocol buffer shares the picture buffer when the explicitly selected demodulator is already narrow. Filter state, protocol bins/candidates, at most one PD chroma pair, and fixed-size authoritative image rows are retained. Image state is bounded by each mode's geometry, up to 640x496. The streaming process does not retain full raw recordings indefinitely. Processing is proportional to new audio, with optional per-channel fits; no repeated whole-recording decode or end-of-image refinement is required.
